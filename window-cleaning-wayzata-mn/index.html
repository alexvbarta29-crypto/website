    We know Wayzata’s windows

    Wayzata wraps around Wayzata Bay at the northeast tip of Lake Minnetonka, and most of what makes the town special is something you take in through glass: the view down the bay from Lake Street, the 1906 Depot at the end of the Lakewalk, the swimmers at Wayzata Beach in July. Wayzata’s homes run from the lake estates along Ferndale Road and Grays Bay to the mix of older homes and new builds in Old Holdridge on the east side, with condos downtown at the Promenade.

    Our crew comes over from Delano on Highway 12, about 20 minutes away, so Wayzata is a regular stop rather than a special trip. That includes the addresses that carry a Wayzata ZIP but technically sit in Orono or Minnetonka. We clean exterior and interior glass, wash screens, clear gutters, and pressure- or soft-wash siding: everything on the outside of a house that a sprinkler head, a row of maples, or a spring of pollen can leave its mark on.

    Lakefront glass

    What’s different about windows in Wayzata

    The lake homes here are built for the view. Floor-to-ceiling picture windows, walls of sliders opening onto the deck, glass that faces the water all day. That is exactly the glass that shows every water spot and pollen streak, and it is the hardest to keep clean. And with older lake homes steadily being replaced by much larger new builds, there is more of that glass every year.

    Very hard water

    Wayzata’s city water is very hard (local water-treatment companies put it around 18 to 24 grains per gallon), and the city’s own drinking-water report notes that iron and manganese can cause staining. Sprinkler overspray dries into mineral rings on lake-facing glass. Our water-fed poles rinse with purified water, so we don’t leave new spots behind, and our hard-water stain removal can lift the ones already there.

    Big, high, and hard to reach

    Two-story great-room windows, transoms over the sliders, glass above a walkout. Water-fed poles let us reach most of it from the ground, which keeps ladders out of your flower beds, and we bring one for the spots that still need it.

    Tree cover and pollen

    The estate lots are old and wooded, and the sugar maples and basswood of the Big Woods Preserve are a reminder of what grew here first. That means pollen in spring, sap and shade in summer, and glass that dries slowly and shows it. Regular cleanings keep up with it.

    Wayzata window cleaning FAQs

    How often should I get my windows cleaned in Wayzata?
    We recommend four cleanings a year to keep lake-facing glass consistently clear, and at minimum twice a year: once in late spring, after the ice is off the bay and the pollen has settled, and again in early fall so the view stays clear through winter. Homes where sprinklers hit the glass get the most out of the quarterly schedule.
    Do you clean screens?
    Yes. Screen cleaning is an add-on to any window cleaning: we take each screen down, hand-wash both sides and the frame, and put it back in its own opening, so you’re not looking at the lake through a layer of dust. We can detail the window tracks on the same visit.
    Can you remove the hard-water spots on my lake-facing windows?
    Usually. Light spotting comes off with a regular cleaning. Heavier mineral buildup needs our hard-water stain removal, a separate treatment we quote after assessing your glass. If years of spotting have etched the glass itself, we’ll tell you honestly how much clarity can come back before we start. The best prevention is redirecting the sprinkler head that’s hitting it.
    How do quotes work?
    They’re free and no-obligation. Call (763) 314-3400 or fill out the quote form and tell us which services you want. Pricing is based on the size of your home, the number of windows, and how easy they are to reach, and you get a clear, upfront, all-in price before any work begins: no hidden fees.
